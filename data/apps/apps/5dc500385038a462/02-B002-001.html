1 The revenge of the monkeys.
 
I will tell you what happened once behind these hills thousands and thousands of winters ago, when our territories were covered with countless pools and lakes. This was in the infancy of the world and our grandparents roamed the savannah, predators, as fast as hares and hornbills.
This was back when our ancestors had not yet conquered the entire bush. These landscapes were infested with wild animals, and the great apes, our bush brothers, the cynocephalus, had forged a great kingdom that covered the Sanguié and its heights. But this was in ancient times.
A hunter, the most skilful ever known, had established himself on the edge of these domains. All he had to do was to throw an object at his target; it fell dead. He was called "the one who never missed".
He had a powerful pack of three hundred and thirty-three greyhounds ready for anything: game or death. They would track their prey at unsuspected distances and jump to the height of his hair to rid him of the adventurous flies that inhabit the bush and always follow the hunters and the carrion.
He was always accompanied by his faithful companions: one hundred and sixty-six on the right, one hundred and sixty-six on the left, with the leader of the Mamina band in the lead. When they entered the bush, each one returned with his mouth agape and his prey in his teeth.
Behind his house, the bones of the victims piled up, rose, rose so high that they rivalled Mount Sanguié. They were monkey bones: and at night they glittered in the darkness like the little lights that escape from cemeteries.
In the bush, every birth had this mournful ceremony: Father-Cynocephalus took the newborn and turned his gaze towards the East:
"Look, look, he would say, on the side where the sun rises. There is the home of Man; there is your tomb, for here we die no longer old. There is the son of Death, he killed the fathers of your fathers, the grandparents of your grandparents.
And Cynocephalus-newborn gazed at the distant phosphorus. And Mother-Cynocephalus shed tears, abundant tears.
Every day the hunter entered the bush, killed, ravaged, and returned. The terror of the hounds!
The numbers began to dwindle seriously and Chief-Cynocephalus ordered that the master of the drums alert the kingdom. That day, the cynocephalus in a council of war decided to file their teeth, to exercise their jaws, to train the youngest lookouts on all the cailcédrats of the kingdom at the edge of the virgin forest. But the hunter always returned to the kingdom with three hundred and thirty-three greyhounds ready for anything. They would spot the scouting monkeys at unsuspected distances and wreak havoc.
Then the mournful drums sounded again in the forest and the hounds decided what they would do.
One day, at the bend in a hunt, "the man who never missed" flinched, he who had never flinched in his life. What did he see? One of those beautiful women who do not exist on earth and who very rarely touch the imagination of men. And immediately his life was changed. He forgot about the hunt and returned home. The leader of the band found the whole affair most bizarre but said nothing. He turned around after his master, tongue-tied, dead-eyed, his companions with him.
From that day on, our man had no respite. He bent over backwards to satisfy his demands. The greatest and most painful of these was to kill his best friends, the dogs, every day.
Three hundred and thirty-three days had passed now. All the dogs had been killed. "He who never failed" had sacrificed them all on the altar of love. He loved his wife so much that he had slaughtered her faithful dog Mamina despite her mother's opposition.
One morning, the hunter thought, now that I have killed all my friends, what will she ask me? And she did call him.
"What do you want from me? - You who have never missed, do you know the big cheese tree in the middle of the bush? It's his sauce I want to eat tonight. Go and bring me the flowers of this giant.
The hunter knew this tree well. It was the biggest tree in the forest. He knew it well because he had killed the most vigorous of the lumberjacks under its shade.
Go there," she said, "I will go with you.
He set off without delay and the little caravan disappeared into the distance. They arrived at the foot of the big cheese tree. He began to climb and climb. He indexed some young flowers. She refused them. He climbed again, again and again. At the foot of the giant, she watched. He threw a young flower. She said no. He attacked again, he was now flying in the air. He could see the whole bush and his eyes took in the landscape. For a moment he imagined himself in a dizzying fall. A cold sweat dampened his armpits. Now he reached the top and the tree began to sway, to sway. At this point there was the last flower of the cheese tree, the very first to gather the sun's rays before the others. The giant son of the forest shuddered and the top wavered, ready to break. He asked if this flower was suitable.
At the foot of the giant, the flower, which had become a black dot, began to cry out. It screamed so loudly that the bowels of the bush shuddered. She metamorphosed and suddenly became a monkey. It screamed again. Then all the monkey people who lived in the whole bush came running. The older ones also stood up, flint in their left hands, arrows and daggers in their right hands; filing and walking. They also had pouches slung over their shoulders in which they dipped the slender javelins to soak them in deadly powder.
In a flash, they swarmed to the bottom of the forest giant to witness the end of the enemy of the race. To the sound of the tom-tom, the monkeys cut and cut the cheese tree. Our man picked up his flute as he used to call his friends the dogs. called Mamina:
-Mamina o Mamina o Kun kun biolé... called, called again. From the depths of her home, the man's mother had heard her son. Something was threatening his life. She took a pouch of potion. She poured the contents over the dog bones she had carefully preserved. The bones became big greyhounds pawing and barking. They heard:
- Mamina o Mamina o Kun kum biolé...
In a flash, they too swarmed to the bottom of the giant. To this day, the monkey people have not forgotten this affair: the rout of the avengers. The pack cleared out just as the cheese tree gave way. That day, the dead of the monkey people, the tom-toms, the axes and the weapons of the sons of the bush were not counted.
"He who had never failed" had come down. He was now walking towards his home, far from the bush. His friends accompanied him, bold and panting, proud and faithful as before. They were as before. Three hundred and thirty-three. One hundred and sixty-six on the right; one hundred and sixty-six on the left, Mamina in the lead.
It is from that time that men have learned to prefer friendship to the whims of their hearts.
