2 The oath of the graves.
In those days, machines were not yet barking on the savannah. The days were born on the hills and crumbled on the cliffs over there, towards Sokouraba. At that time, from Ouéléni to Sindou, from Tagbara to Nanergué, the days rose over harvests and fields heavy with grain; the days rose and fell over old men satiated with a hundred years. Sikasso was not yet a wall.
In those days, machines were not yet barking on the savannah. And from Kankalaba to Katiola, from Korogho to Ségou, among the Famas and the Sofas, the farmers and the Foulahs, the smalas and the tatas had heard this tale: Two friends had sworn that if one of them died, the deceased would return to earth to take the living to the land of the dead.
One day, Gnibè died and was buried in the village grove. Time passed, wintering followed wintering, and it seemed as if everything had been forgotten when, one night during the harvest, a sizzling sound of a balafon was heard in the halo. The ghost of Gnibè came down from the hills and the white of his shroud glittered in the shadows of the night, coming closer and closer to the village.
The elders and all the men heard it and the old women trembled in their homes. The ghost said:
- Zan, Zan, my friend Zan. Remember when I was still on earth. You made an oath that if one day one of us died the dead would return to earth to take the living to the land of the dead. If your word is true, take your balafon and follow me.
 
The houses froze like that mystical moment before the Konon leaves the village; then Zan appeared and carrying the xylophone on his shoulder, he answered:
- Gnibè, Gnibè, my friend Gnibè. Yes, it was I, Zan, who, while you were alive, made the sacred oath that if one day one of us died, the deceased would return to earth to take the living to the land of the dead. Take me with you.
The balafons of the two friends perfumed the region with a macabre melody, and the village watched sobbing as the faithful man left, now rolling down the hill towards the slope of the tombs. Having reached the threshold of his freshly opened tomb, Gnibè invited Zan to go down. He did so, and when his foot touched the vault at the last moment, Gnibè held him back by the hand:
-The bottom of the vault you are about to step into belongs to the territory of the dead. Come back to the light among the world of the living. You are a true friend. In the realm of the dead I will watch over you. Your life shall be as long as the thread of the cattails; your beard shall cover the ground of your s; your estates shall be covered with harvest and your children as numerous as sesame seeds. Everywhere, men in immense procession will come to your home.
You will return to the village of men. You will say to the elders: "Burn a white rooster on this tomb. In the land of the dead I will watch over you.
But that was a long time ago, when the white feathers of friendship covered the graves. In those days, machines did not yet bark on the savannah.
