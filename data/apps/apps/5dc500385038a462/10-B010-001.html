5 Mba Soaamba the Hare and Mba Nanga the Scorpion.
In the past, Mba Soaamba the long-eared hare and Mba Nanga the scorpion, whose tail ends in a poisonous sting, had a very good relationship as neighbours. They helped each other and shared their joys and sorrows.
But, as in those days, animals could marry men's daughters, Mba Soaamba was engaged to Amsatou, a very beautiful girl with a bewitching smile. He frequently went alone to visit his parents-in-law, bringing them large bundles of dry wood or large red or white cola nuts.
One evening, in all good faith, Mba Soaamba went to Mba Nanga and said to him:
- Neighbour, my fiancée Amsatou will soon be twenty-one. I intend to go and negotiate the date of the wedding with her parents-in-law. Since in such circumstances, out of respect and courtesy, the fiancé cannot speak directly with the girl's parents, I would like you to come with me tomorrow morning. You will be the main negotiator of this marriage agreement.
- No problem," replied Mba Nanga. You can count on my constant availability whenever necessary.
So Mba Soaamba left his neighbour and went back to his home. He made the final preparations for departure. The next day, early in the morning, the two animals took the path leading to the men's village. They arrived at the banks of a raw river. For the crossing, each passenger had to pay the ferryman five cauris. Then Mba Nanga made the following proposal to his neighbour.
- Neighbour and friend Mba Soaamba! We have to pay the ferryman ten cauris. I have an idea; if we use it, we will save five cauris, which will allow us to increase the gesture we will make to our parents-in-law.
- Very interesting and brilliant idea, neighbour?
Very, very simple. As I am very small, you let me lodge in one of your ears. The ferryman will take you across without being aware of the presence of two passengers on the ferry.
- An excellent proposal. Put it in my left ear without delay.
Mba Soaamba. The latter paid the five cauris demanded by the ferryman. Everything went well and the two animals were on the other side of the river. Mba Soaamba asked his companion to come out of his shelter to continue the journey. Mba Nanga decided that it was not yet time and insinuated:
- Friend Soaamba, keep me in your ear; we will play a joke on your father-in-law and he will hasten the wedding at the expense of the other wealthy competitors.
In spite of himself, Mba Soaamba complied with his neighbour's wishes. When he arrived at his parents-in-law's house, he asked the scorpion in a low voice to come out for the customary greetings, but the latter whispered:
- Neighbour, have you no memory? Have you forgotten my second proposal? - No!" replied Mba Soaamba. Only I'm beginning to worry about the sincerity of your words. Are you sure that this prank will not work against us?
- Good or bad joke, Soaamba, you will do what I order you to do.
- Neighbour Nanga! You are starting to exaggerate. We are at the in-laws' house, but watch your language.
- Soaamba! Don't forget that I am the master of the situation. You will obey me without a murmur.
- How can I do that? You Nanga, obeying you without a murmur? Who gave you this power to challenge me like this?
- Soaamba, the length of your ears does not frighten me. I order you to insult your father-in-law. Tell him he's a lousy fool.
What are you saying Nanga? Have you lost your mind? People were right to say that you have occasional fits of madness.
- Soaamba! You call me crazy? Good for you! "If the water canary you wear on your head breaks, you should gladly wash yourself with its water". Then, as I am mad, I will act mad. Old cockroach, they say that with your long ears you are the most intelligent of the animals, but will you tell me today which of us is the stronger, and therefore the more intelligent?
- Nanga, can you tell me the origin of this implacable hatred and why did you wait until now to explode?
- Soaamba, hatred or not, I order you to insult your father-in-law.
- Nanga, come what may, I won't do it. I am ready to endure any suffering.
- We'll see, Mr. Brave and Intelligent Animal. It really takes a brave man to marry the beautiful Amsatou. Since you refuse to carry out my orders, a little venom from my sting will measure your foolish temerity.
Mba Nanga thrust his sting into the auricle of Mba Soaamba's ear, who could not contain the excruciating pain.
- Ouch! My ear! My ear! Neighbour, you are a fool, a murderer of the worst kind. May God curse you.
- Soaamba ear, do you still have the spirit to insult me? Let's push the little sting into the tender membrane even harder.
A shudder of pain and fury shook Mba Soaamba's face. The son-in-law shouted loudly, very loudly.
- Ouch! Ouch! My ear is burning! My ear is on fire.
Mba Nanga, master of the situation, ordered Mba Soaamba to insult his father-in-law, his mother-in-law and his fiancée. The order was carried out. The hare pointed out that his father-in-law is a lousy fool, that his mother-in-law is a lousy carrion, that his fiancée Amsatou is a bastard with owl eyes. This irritated the father-in-law. Here is a shameless, if not mad, son-in-law. He insults us and treats us, the princes of the blood, like descendants of slaves. Let's kill him, it's him who wanted it.
Well beaten by his parents-in-law, Mba Soaamba found a trick to 'save his skin'. He slumped to the ground and let it out:
- Loving in-laws, blood princes, I owe you respect. Pass on, I say pass on to Amsatou, the beautiful Amsatou, the beautiful of the beautiful that my soul beyond the other world will continue to love her. If fate wills it, we will meet again. Farewell!
Motionless, with his ears folded back to the ground, Mba Soaamba gave no further sign of life. The parents-in-law dragged him to a corner of the compound, waiting for the favours of the night to abandon the corpse of their son-in-law at the crossroads. At that moment Mba Nanga, sure of his success, came out of his shelter. He headed towards the hare's parents-in-law, convinced that he had won the heart of the adorable Amsatou. He thought he had eliminated his rival Soaamba for good. He was astonished to see the long-eared animal hopping on its two hind legs. He grabbed a large stone and without comment crushed Mba Nanga the scorpion. He then explained to his father-in-law the trickery of his wicked and hypocritical neighbour. The father of the beautiful Amsatou understood the wickedness of the scorpion. Furious, he took the same large stone and crushed the little animal for a second time, cursing it: "Wicked creature, this is the fate that will be reserved for all your descendants from now on".
The men got together and granted the hand of their daughter Amsatou to Mba Soaamba the long-eared animal, who celebrated a double victory: the victory of having succeeded in thwarting the diabolical calculations of his neighbour Nanga the scorpion, and the victory of having married Amsatou, the daughter of men, who moved all the competitors by her gracefulness, the delicacy of her complexion and the indeterminacy of her gaze.
But since then, Mba Soaamba has become very suspicious, because he says to himself that in human relationships, when the charming being interferes, true friends are rare.
