8 The magic horse.
 
Once upon a time, a man and his two wives lived in a village. The first had two children and the second had only one. The man fell ill. He suffered for days, and more and more excruciatingly as time went by. One day, feeling that his end was near, he sent for his family members to give them his last advice. He said to them, "I am happily heading towards the end of my days. After my death my children, love one another. Help each other and sympathise with your loved ones in their misfortunes. Make these two women your mothers and respect them as such. As for you my wives, love these children and consider them as your own children. I thank you all for your faithfulness.
After having spoken thus, he fell silent and as agreed, he died. Unfortunately, he was so poor that the children were not able to organise his funeral and feed themselves until the rainy season. They decided to seek their fortune elsewhere, leaving their mothers with the little grain they had. After several hours of walking, they came to a place where the road split into three. They separated, each taking one lane. But before they did so, they made it clear that they had to meet under the big tree that stood nearby at the first rain. They set off.
The day after the first rain, they met under the tree. The elder one said: "My trip really didn't work out. I only brought back gris-gris.
So did I," exclaimed the younger brother, and the younger brother said the same thing. They decided to experiment with their products.
The older brother kicked the ground and a well appeared. The second brother tilted it like a canary to remove the water. The little brother stroked the top and made it disappear.
Clearly, no one was bringing back anything that would put them out of their misery. At home and after a good rest, the younger brother sent for his older brothers and said. "Of the gris-gris I brought back, one is in my bag and can help us get anything we want. But great caution, sincerity and honesty are required on both sides, between us of course. Indeed, thanks to this last gris-gris, I can become a horse if not horses because the colours I will have after each transformation will have nothing in common with the previous ones. Thus, you will sell me and each time, I will come back. Avoid selling me to a one-eyed marabout.
No sooner said than done, and they began to amass a fortune thanks to the magic horse. In honour of their father, they organised a grand funeral. When the ceremony was over, they sold the horse to fill the gap it had created. They became the richest and happiest people in the village. Jealous of seeing the little brother benefit from these riches, the two brothers from the same mother got together and went to sell the horse to a one-eyed marabout who already knew the horse's power. He had a round hut built around him and the containers in which he put his food, leaving only a small opening through which he gave him food and drink. Three months passed.
The two brothers said that he would not return and turned his mother into a labourer, almost a slave. Every morning, from the first crow of the cock, she had to sweep the yard and bring water to the animals. She ate very little. The day she failed to get up early, they insulted her as if she were a child at fault.
For three years, the child was in this unwanted hut. One day in the third year, the marabout went to the market. Taking advantage of his father's absence, the marabout's boy widened the opening and let the horse out. Saddling it, he jumped on it quickly. Trotting gently, the horse moved away from the courtyard and then, in a surprisingly spirited manner, jumped up, knocked down the boy and ran away.
From the market, the marabout noticed. Like the wind, he set off after the horse. Seeing that the distance between them was decreasing considerably, the runaway turned into a turtle-dove to go faster, but he was joined by a sparrowhawk who was only the marabout. Passing over the court of a chief, the turtle-dove changed into a ring and fell into the pocket of the Naba's (Chief's) big boubou without him realising it. Becoming a griot, the marabout came to the chief singing his praises. The latter gave him rich gifts, but he refused, saying that he wanted what was in his pocket. Not understanding anything, the chief thought that the gifts were meagre and he increased them, but the griot persisted in his refusal. The chief plunged his hand into his pocket and took out a silver ring. Without trying to understand, he handed it to the griot who thanked him profusely as he walked away. A few meters away, he stopped, gathered ten goat skins, put the ring inside and sewed the openings. The ring turned into a mouse and set to work gnawing on them. When he had made a small hole in the tenth one, it became small millet and began to flow. Unfortunately, as the last grain fell, it touched the heel of the one-eyed man who turned around. He saw these grains and turned into a chicken to peck at them. When she wanted to pick up the last one, he jumped back once, twice and the third time he found himself far from the hen. There he changed into a cat, jumped on the chicken and killed it.
Reverting to his human form, he left for his father's yard where he arrived in the middle of the night when everyone was sleeping. Without announcing himself, he entered his mother's hut, who woke up suddenly. Trembling with all her body, she asked: "Who are you? and what do you want from me?
It's a stranger," replied the child in a reassuring voice.
- Come out, or the boys will hurt me badly when they see you.
Shocked, the child made himself known before asking her to tell him what was happening here after him. She told him everything. He then told her not to leave her hut in the morning.
At dawn, the younger man, not having seen the manoeuvre outside, began to speak rude and offensive words against her, making threats and insults. As he approached, he saw the child's back. Not recognising him, he asked: "Do you enjoy receiving strangers in your hut without our knowledge? Wait and see". He wanted to enter the hut when the young brother said: "This is what you are doing to my mother in my absence! The latter left in a hurry to announce the return of this brother to his mother and to the elder of the court. The young brother left his mother's hut and invited the others to join him. When they were all together, he asked them: "Have you forgotten what our father told us when he died? And what I told you before I turned into a horse. You have betrayed me, therefore to avenge me, brothers, I am turning you into dogs and your mother into a donkey.
The two dogs guarded the yard and the animals. The donkey helped him in his various jobs. He left these three people in their state for three years before turning them into people. Since then, they lived happily and were not harmed.
Everything on earth is organised in such a way that everyone has his own account. If, out of jealousy, greed or dishonesty, some want to enjoy the fruits of everyone else's labour for themselves, it goes without saying that there will be justice and punishment. Nothing is done at random in our country. A challenge to those who are as permeable to great wealth as sand is to water.
