9 Forgiveness.
Once there was a rich man. He was very happy with his wife and three children: a boy and two girls. He had a servant who worked in the fields, who also did all the housework; he washed the clothes, the kitchen utensils, and shopped very sparingly.
Our man was very mean. He frequently got angry and beat the servant for the slightest peccadillo. A tireless worker but excessively mistreated, the boy lived miserably. He did not eat enough. He got up early in the morning and went to bed late at night. He slept on the ground near the sheepfold. All he had for clothing was a briefs and an indigo blanket used during the cold winter nights. The man's three children, on the other hand, spent all their time playing.
One day, fed up with hunger, the servant stole an ear of dry corn. Behind the sheepfold, he lit a fire to toast the corn. Unfortunately! A whirlwind suddenly surrounded the compound and scattered the embers over the straw seckos, which took pleasure in spreading the flames to the roofs of the huts. The whole concession became a glowing inferno.
Helplessly, the servant running here and there could not stop the capricious impulses of the fire. The smoke rose straight up and high into the sky. All the neighbours, alerted, rushed to put out the burning thatched roofs. The man who was in the cabaret also ran to join the men and women in putting out the fire. Despite the obvious will of the inhabitants, the concession burned and no material goods were spared. No human life was lost, but more wealth: millet granaries, jars containing clothes and jewellery, everything was consumed.
While the ashes smoked, the servant took advantage of the hubbub and fled. This absence attracted the attention of the people who soon discovered the half-charred ear of corn. The audience concluded that the servant was definitely the one who had set the fire. The man was furious and swore that he would whip him to death.
That evening and in the following days, all the neighbours, relatives and friends came to the aid of the stricken family. The men re-roofed the huts. The women, who were more sensitive, brought millet, maize, groundnuts, okra and a few strips of cotton fabric to make clothes for the children.
After a week, the man went to his trusted neighbour and said: - Neighbour, give me back the golden canary that I entrusted to you twenty years ago. The neighbour replied:
- Have you gone mad? A canary full of gold! If your servant burnt your plot, I don't think you have lost your mind. The man replied:
- The only witness, on the evening of the handing over of the canary full of gold was Wẽnnaam (God).
- If your Wẽnnaam can testify, let him come forward, retorted the neighbour. The man became angry and like a wounded lion, he roared and struck the hypocritical neighbour to death. Immediately he was arrested and imprisoned.
The wife and three children were abandoned and ended up begging from door to door for a living. But one day the woman complained that she couldn't take it anymore:
- If I were alone, I would suffer less. Death came to her rescue and took the three children. Then, losing her mind, she went naked through the village; she ended up throwing herself into a river where she drowned.
The story doesn't end there, because the judges had meanwhile dealt with the case of the golden canary. They found that the man had indeed entrusted a canary full of gold to his neighbour. Released, the man asked about his family. The adults remained silent. Only the cheeky children agreed to answer the man's questions.
- And my wife'?
- Drowned," said the children.
- And my children?
- All dead," the children replied again without a feeling of embarrassment. Then, taking his head in both hands, his eyes dry, his gaze fixed on nothing, he said nothing more. Silence did the rest until the day when the servant was seen at his master's house. He knelt before the man with tears in his eyes and pleaded:
- Master, the truth stings, but it shines like the sun. I was the one who set the fire which destroyed all your property. I fled to avoid corporal punishment, but day and night my conscience was scolding me. So I came to you. Do with me what you will.
The man kept a long silence, the necessary time for reflection and meditation. After this dialogue of the deaf without gesture, the man decided to speak:
- Domestic, you are not the culprit. You tried to roast a dry corncob to appease your hunger, and the whirlwind caused the fire that burnt down my entire compound. But was it you who beat my neighbour to death? And my wife? And my children? All these crimes are not your doing. Come on, servant, I forgive you.
The servant immediately felt relieved. He threw himself into his master's arms and said:
- Thank you! Thank you, thank you! For the rest of my life I will never forget what you have just given me: forgiveness. The man, moved, with tears in his eyes, murmured:
- Poor servant! You say thank you to me, thank you to me who was rich and has now lost everything? What can a beggar or a wretch give? Why did you wait so long before coming to ask my forgiveness?
- The servant explained:
- Master, time dictates its laws to men. The day after the fire, if I came to you, I was sure that I would never have been forgiven. You were rich and did not care about the suffering of others. The man replied:
- Indeed, the day after the fire, if you had come forward, I would have beaten you to death like a rabid dog. Now, suffering has softened my heart and I resolve to preach forgiveness, to seek words of comfort for all those who suffer and toil in life.
Our fallen rich man no longer saw the boy as a servant to be exploited as a beast of burden, but as a brother. Together they decided to rebuild their lives, to work on an equal basis to achieve happiness.
