11 The old woman and the old cow.
A long time ago there lived in a sad cottage an old woman. Her only possession was an old cow, which she took good care of. To people who asked her why she didn't sell the animal to finish her old age, she answered:
- It is not yet time to sell my old cow. The day you see me going to the market, know that I am about to leave for the "other world" (the world after death). The people, intrigued by this answer and eager to discover the secret of the old woman's thoughts, persisted with their original question, saying
- Old woman, what use would be the money from the sale of this animal since you leave no heir. It is better to live your life to the fullest here. At this new question, the old woman found pleasure in developing her idea.
- Good people, human life is comparable to a river with waters that are sometimes gentle and peaceful, sometimes furious and turbulent in times of flood. I have known this gentle life when my husband and children were alive. The sad and troubled life, I am living in solitude. But life, like the water of a river, must continue its course, overcome obstacles and flow into the sea of the afterlife. Indignant at such reasoning, people replied.
-. Old woman, today's panties are better than tomorrow's baggy trousers. Save that money for the gravediggers who will take you to your final resting place. The old woman continued:
-A man of good character, would you rather be happy in youth, unhappy in old age, or suffer through a flourishing youth and enjoy an old age of peace and quiet? It is all a question of choice. Is the prodigal wrong to overspend, to squander his wealth? Is the miser wrong to want to save excessively?
I have chosen to prepare my passage to the other side of the grave and I think that with the money from the sale of my old cow, I will make my way to the luminous Kingdom of heaven. As for the young people who are going to dig my grave, I know that all work deserves a salary. Also, you will find in my jars millet which will be used to prepare "Zom-Kom and tô". If that is not enough, let me rot if you don't mind, since the death of my dear husband and children, how many people think of giving me a small cola nut? On the contrary, I am called a "witch or a soul eater" and therefore the author of the death of all my loved ones. In spite of my advanced age, I make the pond, look for dead wood, cook, cultivate my millet, okra or peanut fields, repair the roof of my hut, look after my old cow.
The raw truth, the bitter truth, people ended up leaving the old woman alone.
And so, every day, they watched the old woman come and go, pulling her old cow by a small rope. But one day people saw the old woman leading her favourite animal to the market on the other side of the Kul-Wooko River. She arrived at the river bank and the old cow refused to enter the water. The old woman went after the old cow, but it was no use. Then, discouraged, she called on a dog that was passing by
Dog, do me a favour:
- Bite my old cow here. She will cross the Kul-Wooko river, I will sell her to the butcher. With the money I will make my way to the Kingdom of Heaven.
The dog answered:
- My dear old woman, I can't. And for good reason, the cow didn't do anything to me. let me continue my hunt.
The old woman stayed with her old cow for a while, when she saw a shepherd's crook leading a herd of sheep and goats by himself. She asked the stick:
- Stick, do me a favour. Hit the dog here, the dog will bite the cow, the cow will cross the Kul-Wooko river. I will sell it to the butcher. With the money I will make my way, the way to the Kingdom of Heaven.
The stick refused.
- My dear old lady, I can't. And for good reason, the dog has done nothing to me. Let me water my flock.
The old woman was disappointed, but she was patient and waited. Termites came along in single file, looking for the clay to build their home. The old woman hastened to ask the termites.
- Termites, do me a favour. Gnaw on the stick here. The stick will hit the dog. The dog will bite the cow. The cow will cross the Kul-Wooko river. I will sell it to the butcher. With the money I will make my way, the way to the Kingdom of Heaven.
The termites refused.
- My dear old woman, we cannot. And for good reason, the stick has done nothing to us. Let us carry our clay.
The old woman became irritated when she saw a rooster approaching:
- Rooster, do me a favour. Peck the termites here. The termites will gnaw on the stick the stick will hit the dog. The dog will bite the cow. The cow will cross the Kul-Wooko river. I will sell it to the butcher. With the money I will make my way, the way to the Kingdom of Heaven.
The cock threw a coco-rico and said:
- My dear old lady, I can't. And for good reason, the termites have done nothing to me. Let me crow at the top of my voice.
Discouraged, the old woman was about to sit down when she saw a snake, do me a favour. Bite the proud rooster here. The proud rooster will peck at the ugly termites. The ugly termites will gnaw on the weak stick. The cowardly stick will hit the cowardly dog. The cowardly dog will bite the old cow The old cow will cross the Kul-Wooko River. I will sell it to the old chief butcher. With the money I will make my way, the way to the Kingdom of Heaven.
The snake bit the rooster, which hurriedly pecked at the termites, which in turn gnawed at the stick, which attacked the dog, which bit the cow's hocks, forcing the cow to cross the Kul-Wooko River.
The old woman, on the other bank, thanked the snake. She sold the old cow to the old chief butcher. She returned to her hut singing: "The air of the living world is enough for me. Today, I will say goodbye to the world of the living. I will happily take the road to the kingdom of heaven. In the kingdom of heaven, joy and bliss are eternal.
People were surprised by the overflowing joy of the old woman who had returned from the market without her favourite animal. The time did not pass. The next morning, the old woman was not seen again. The inhabitants realised that she had indeed left this world. So they mobilised to dig the grave. When it was time to bury her, the gravedigger went down into the grave first. He lay down on his back and received the mortal remains on his chest and then laid them gently in a corner set aside for this purpose. At that moment, the gravedigger was blinded by a light of unprecedented clarity. He then saw strange beings come up to the old woman and ask her if she would be willing to pay for the journey to the Kingdom of Heaven. The old woman, swaddled in a black loincloth, stirred; her arm relaxed, indicating a corner of the loincloth where something was tied. One of the strange beings untied the loincloth, took cowrie shells, cola nuts and tobacco and ordered other beings dressed in white to make a luminous path for the old woman. Immediately the gravedigger saw the old woman take a glittering route amidst cheers and ovations. The gravedigger could not believe his eyes when he saw the great procession moving towards a wonderful palace. Then normal light returned. He was able to leave the pit. The long silence in the black hole made people ask questions:
I almost did. What I saw deserves to be made known to the people.
The burial ceremonies ended late at night. Back in the village, the gravedigger gathered the young, old and elderly and told them what he had witnessed in the old woman's grave.
The men became frightened and from then on a custom was established at funerals. The relatives and friends of the deceased give the gravedigger everything necessary for the person leaving the world of the living to free himself from all the obligations required for passage to the "other world". The living, anxious about the fate of their relatives and whose rites had not been performed, commission the new traveller from beyond the grave to give their relatives something to speed up the process of entering the celestial kingdom. The gravedigger alone acts as an intermediary in this passage and, from generation to generation, the power to bury the dead is passed from father to son.
