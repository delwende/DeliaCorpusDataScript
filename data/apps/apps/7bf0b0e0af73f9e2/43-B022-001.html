22) MANY PEOPLE IN THE BIBLE WERE REFUGEES
The Bible tells of many people who were on the run.
Abraham fled from a famine with his family and all his possessions.
And God went with him and promised to take care of him.
And God kept his promise.
Joseph was sold by his brothers and taken to Egypt by smugglers.
But God was with him and not only provided for Joseph,
but later also for his family.
And even when Jesus was still a small child,
his parents had to flee with him from violence.
Jesus knows the feeling of being persecuted and in danger.
Jesus told his friends:
"I have told you all these things so that in me you may have peace.
Here on earth,
you will experience many difficult things.
But take courage,
for I have overcome the world" (The Bible.
John 16:33).
FAITH,
HOPE,
LOVE
The Bible records how God has travelled through history with mankind.
It contains God's message to mankind.
In the Bible we read that we should love God with all our heart,
with all our soul,
with all our strength and with all our mind,
and that we should love our neighbour as ourselves.
The Bible contains words that comfort and encourage us,
even in the worries and difficulties of our lives.
Christians read the Bible and try to organise their everyday lives accordingly.
Many people have already experienced that God still speaks to them through the Bible today.
They read a passage and suddenly a verse becomes important for them and they realise that God is speaking to them personally.
This is how God teaches,
encourages and comforts them today as in the past.

A STORY THAT JESUS TOLD
"Two men went to the temple to pray.
One was a Pharisee,
the other a tax collector.
The proud Pharisee stood there and prayed:
'I thank you,
God,
that I am not a sinner like other people,
like the robbers and the unjust,
the adulterers or especially like this tax collector!
For I do not cheat anyone,
I do not commit adultery,
I fast twice a week and I regularly give you a tenth of my income".
The tax collector,
on the other hand,
remained standing at a distance,
not even daring to raise his eyes as he prayed:
"O God,
have mercy on me,
for I am a sinner.
" I tell you,
this sinner - and not the Pharisee - returned home as one justified before God.
For whoever exalts himself will be humbled,
but whoever humbles himself will be exalted."
(Luke 18:9-14).
