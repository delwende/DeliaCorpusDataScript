In general,
in the world there are populations reacting to shame (like Asians,
Africans,
etc.)
and those responding to guilt (like Europeans,
Westerners).
Anyone who reacts to a mistake with remorse and feelings of guilt belongs to the culture of guilt.
His conscience pushes him to look for ways to admit his fault and put things in order.
Conversely,
in a culture of shame and dishonor,
it seems that as long as it remains secret,
the transgressor feels no guilt.
But if the transgression is discovered,
it brings shame and a bad conscience.
To escape dishonor,
we therefore seek to conceal our fault.
A bad and a good conscience
In the culture of guilt,
the bad conscience is appeased if,
for the wrongdoer,
justice is reestablished;
on the other hand,
in the culture of shame and dishonor,
it is when the person is considered and honored again.
It quickly became apparent that the importance of honor in the culture of shame and that of justice in the culture of guilt are not only linked to conscience.
In a culture of shame,
reciprocal honor is an absolute law in everyday life.
Any action that contributes to honor is good,
on the other hand any action that brings discredit is reprobated.
We therefore strive to ensure personal honor and that of the family.
Honor is defended at all costs.
What seems to me to be a lie will not be considered morally false,
if honor is preserved and shame avoided?
Nor should the honor of a superior be tarnished.
 
The culture where law and justice are basic values is opposed to the culture oriented towards honor.
Here,
it is not primarily about personal honor or family honor,
and what others think of my actions is secondary.
The important thing is that I know that I acted fairly and that I am right.
To do this,
we need rules and principles that are respected in common life.
For example,
I must be certain that my interlocutor is telling the truth and keeping his promises.
Discussing the rules is not desired,
negotiating compromises is not appreciated.
These two basic structures,
honor in the culture of shame and justice in the culture of guilt,
influence daily community life so strongly that we will henceforth speak of the orientation towards honor and the orientation towards Justice.
If we polarize by distinguishing the two cultures in this way,
it is to better highlight the differences.
There are of course “gray areas”,
justice and honor are not mutually exclusive.
