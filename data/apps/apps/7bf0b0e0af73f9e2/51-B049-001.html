CONSCIENCE IN THE TWO DIFFERENT CULTURES
Awareness of right and wrong,
good and bad.
The difference between orientation towards honor and orientation towards justice already begins in the education of small children.
Every child is born with a conscience.
Through the relationship with its interlocutor,
this consciousness learns what is good and what is bad.
If the interlocutor reacts positively to an action,
it means that it is good;
but if the interlocutor reacts negatively and turns away,
the action is bad.
In a rather individualistic culture,
this interlocutor is the restricted family with few reference people.
They expect the child to learn to independently distinguish truth from falsehood,
good from evil:
“You know we don't do that!
 » Thus,
conscience not only records knowledge of right and wrong,
but also internalizes a “reference person” whose role is to accuse it.
When the child commits a mistake,
this internalized reference person tells him that he behaved badly and he then feels guilty.
This will continue when he is an adult.
On the other hand,
in a culture where the social group plays an important role,
the child has many reference people.
So,
he learns to pay attention to how people react to his behavior.
He constantly hears:
“What will the others say?
 » Thus,
the reference person who accuses is not internalized in consciousness,
the child (later also the adult) is subject to group pressure.
If they accuse him,
he must be ashamed,
because he has become guilty.
In addition,
he also brought opprobrium to his reference people.
When a man commits an act that brings dishonor upon himself,
it falls also upon his wife,
his children,
and all his friends.
In such a culture,
people try to hide their transgressions in order to escape dishonor.
It is impossible to voluntarily admit fault.
The fault is denied for as long as possible.
But since the distinction between good and evil is clearly present in the conscience,
the guilty person fears being discovered.
This fear is often so strong that he or she avoids anyone who might make an accusation in order to avoid disgrace.
This means that,
depending on the circumstances,
that person might end friendships or move to another village.
In summary,
we see that the consciousness of every human being,
regardless of the culture in which they grow up,
internalizes what is considered good or bad,
as right or wrong.
It protects him from mistakes and helps maintain harmonious and peaceful relationships.
After a transgression of norms or rules,
the conscience reacts with feelings of guilt in one culture,
with fear of dishonor in the other.
Liberation will be sought through confession in one culture,
through silence and concealment in the other.
 
Summary
ORIENTATION TOWARDS HONOR
The group determines the standard
Honorable relationships are important
Reference people are outside
When there is a Transgression
- we are afraid of dishonor
- and we try to hide,
conceal
 
ORIENTATION TOWARDS JUSTICE
The individual determines the standard
Correct behavior is important
The reference person is internalized
When there is a Transgression
- we have feelings of guilt
- we aspire to confession
