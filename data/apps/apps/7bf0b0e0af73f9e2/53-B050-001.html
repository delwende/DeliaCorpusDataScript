How is trust born?
In a culture which seeks above all to reduce uncertainties as much as possible (this is the case in European countries),
we first demand that people respect the rules.
In this culture,
you don't build relationships until you're sure you can trust them.
Conversely,
in African and Oriental cultures,
contact is established quickly,
motivated by interest in what is foreign.
This is why hospitality is so important.
Then,
the relationship strengthens as we get to know each other,
and it is only after this approach that we see in which areas we can trust.
Trust is built on relationship,
not the other way around.
This is where the attempt to create contact with asylum seekers fails.
Europeans say to themselves:
“How can I establish a relationship when I don’t know if his story is true?
For fear of being disappointed,
I prefer to be wary.”
For his part,
the asylum seeker says to himself:
“How can I tell you my story if I don’t know you and I’m not sure that you won’t report me?”
So how can we build trusting relationships?
Here are some recipes:
  • Do not set rules as a prerequisite.
  • Be interested in others.
  • Seek contact:
it costs nothing except time,
but this time is found later when a problem arises.
It's not very revolutionary,
but what is essential is not to first expect everything from the other,
but to take the first step.
Trust is a precious thing that can only be given with caution and wisdom.
