Addressing Problems
Putting on a good face and avoiding shame has great value in many cultures,
because it expresses respect for others and for oneself.
This is why people from cultures sensitive to honor prefer not to talk about problems so that the other person does not lose face.
But in everyday life,
problems rarely resolve themselves.
For example,
in rental properties,
the common laundry room is a place that has the potential to kill harmony.
An Asian woman told us that an African neighbor reproached her about the state of the laundry room after use.
Unfortunately,
the African became very angry.
Subsequently,
she was ashamed of it,
so much that she no longer dared to speak to the Asian.
Since that day,
contact has been through the Asian husband.
The two neighbors come from cultures that seek respect,
but their cultures are very different.
For these two women,
it was difficult to take the step and address the conflict head on.
The real problem was no longer the cleanliness of the laundry room,
but the risk of losing face.
