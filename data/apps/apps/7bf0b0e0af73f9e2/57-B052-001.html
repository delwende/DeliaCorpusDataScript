Mediation
How to resolve conflicts when you don't know the culture from the inside?
Fortunately,
there is a way:
mediation.
What are the characteristics of a good mediator?
He must have the trust of both parties,
or at least be respected.
And he must understand the problem,
without being too directly involved.
For a European,
all this requires revising one's conceptions.
Europeans assume that the guilty party must ask for forgiveness.
In a context of honor,
this seems insolent and hurtful.
“He dares to appear before me after everything that happened?»
The culprit shows that he regrets,
precisely by the fact that he resorts to a mediator.
This is not a loophole,
as one might think from a European perspective.
Involving a mediator greatly relieves the person in difficulty.
The disadvantage of this procedure is that it is time consuming.
But isn't it better to waste a little time at the start to avoid ending up in discord after a direct confrontation?
Where can we find capable mediators?
The circle of people who have intercultural experience is widening:
these are people who have spent a fairly long time abroad,
or first or second-generation migrants who are comfortable in both cultures,
or even people who have knowledge of mediation.
All can learn,
by not avoiding intercultural tensions,
but by seeking constructive solutions,
and thus they qualify as possible mediators.
Without mediation,
there is a risk that each party will seek affirmation and harmony in the most welcoming social group,
and this is often the group with a similar cultural background.
With each failure,
frustration increases,
and the gap between the groups widens.
