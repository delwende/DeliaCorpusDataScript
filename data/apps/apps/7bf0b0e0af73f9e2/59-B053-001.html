Offer concrete help
When tensions arise,
mediators can offer concrete help to people sensitive to honor,
for example support in relations with authorities.
The mediator can explain to the court how the problem is felt by the honor-sensitive party.
In a context of fault,
the mediator can already express the fact that the wrongdoer regrets.
From then on,
he only has to briefly confess his fault,
avoiding the entire preparatory process,
which is particularly humiliating.
For people sensitive to honor,
it is also difficult to express criticism,
because they want to avoid the other person losing face.
Here again,
the mediator can take the opportunity to clarify the situation by transmitting justified criticism in a measured and appropriate manner,
so that improvements can be implemented.
This may be necessary between two generations of migrants.
Indeed,
parents and children do not have the same appreciation of things related to the culture of honor and the culture of right (or duty) and there,
mediation can facilitate understanding.
Mediators are very useful in resolving administrative matters during the integration phase.
When you're short on money,
bills are humiliating.
When you fear the rejection of a request,
each official letter is a source of anxiety.
The accompanying person can help a migrant not to forget an important deadline.
If relationships are not limited to administrative matters,
a feeling of belonging can develop,
which is very important for people sensitive to consideration.
