Personal intercultural contacts
Harmony in intercultural contacts is not automatic because cultural reflexes and behaviors are often very different.
We will now give some concrete indications that people from a culture where rule dominates should pay attention to when they are in contact with people from a culture of honor,
and vice versa.
On both sides,
it is important to guard against hasty judgments and things left unsaid.
We have to ask the question why are you doing this?
In this way we will be able to understand the logic of the interlocutor and discover his methods.
For Europeans:
understanding people from another country
It is very important to make people feel accepted and valued.
The feeling of shame comes from feeling excluded from a community.
This is what happens with migrants.
The best antidote is to assure strangers that they belong.
How to create trust?
The best solution is to put yourself in the place of the interlocutor.
People will open up more easily if we show them a sincere willingness to get to know them and if we avoid topics that are perceived as threatening.
Being interested in the country of origin and the family allows them to build relationships through cultural values that they hold dear,
and helps to touch the heart.
Questions like “Why are you here?”
How did you come?
 » provoke distrust,
they are reminiscent of interviews with migrants.
Later,
if the relationship develops,
we can talk about these topics when the atmosphere is relaxed.
Hospitality creates consideration,
but each culture must be respected,
and in different ways.
For example,
in Africa,
we honor our friends by traveling to see them;
in Europe,
we do this by opening our homes and thereby our private sphere.
It is worth asking what form hospitality takes in culture of the migrant and how it communicates consideration,
to try to find a meaningful point to begin the relationship.
 
Before inviting people,
it is often useful to visit them at home,
it shows them that they are important.
Even after many years,
many foreigners have never seen a European interior and they feel this as rejection.
An invitation to a local family is therefore all the more appreciated.
In cultures sensitive to honor,
we avoid expressing criticism directly as much as possible,
so as not to shock anyone.
For example,
after a party,
before saying that something was not put away properly,
you can kindly inquire whether the party went well,
and be understanding,
citing fatigue at the end of the evening.
It is important to avoid generalizing.
Remarks like:
“The kitchen was not clean” are disproportionate when only one corner of the kitchen had been forgotten.
Good communication is most successful if the immediate reaction of discontent is dissipated.
To resolve more complicated issues,
it is worth using a mediator.
In intercultural contacts there will always be disappointments.
This is due to the different expectations of everyone,
but also to the fact that there are people in the world with difficult personalities,
both locals and migrants.
It is wise not to set the bar too high for compliance with Western standards,
at least initially - foreigners are unfamiliar with them - and thus leave room for positive developments.
