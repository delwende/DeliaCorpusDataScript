For foreigners:
understanding Europeans
Among Europeans,
the rule is that the newcomer introduces himself to his neighbors and thus signals his interest in the existing community and his intention to integrate into it.
Spontaneous visits to them are rare,
outside the immediate neighborhood or between close friends.
Indeed,
the inhabitants of Central Europe appreciate being able to prepare their private sphere for visitors.
If you want to establish contact,
the rule is to invite them.
 
Some Europeans want to make no mistakes in intercultural contacts.
This is why a foreigner who wants to invite them to his home for an event can erase a feeling of insecurity by preparing his guests for the visit and explaining to them what he expects of them.
Trust is built when we communicate clearly.
If we cannot respect a commitment,
it is better to say so than to remain silent out of shame:
silence would be a sign that we cannot be trusted.
The frankness of many Europeans can cause hurt.
Often,
they are not intentional,
and sometimes the author is not aware of them.
